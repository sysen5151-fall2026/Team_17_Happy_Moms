Urgent help

If something feels wrong, calling is the right move. Providers would far rather hear from you and find nothing than have you wait it out.

Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room

For heavy bleeding, severe pain, trouble breathing, chest pain, a seizure, fainting, or any situation that feels life-threatening. If you are more than 20 weeks pregnant, your hospital's labor and delivery unit is usually the right door.

Call 911

Signs that need a provider today

These are the maternal warning signs published by ACOG and the CDC. Call your provider or midwife, or go in, rather than logging it and waiting.

Vaginal bleeding, or fluid leaking
Severe or persistent headache
Blurry vision, seeing spots, or light sensitivity
Sudden swelling of the face or hands
Severe belly pain, especially upper abdominal pain
Baby moving less than usual
Fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher
Vomiting that keeps you from holding down fluids
Regular contractions before 37 weeks
Pain, redness, or swelling in one leg
Chest pain, racing heart, or trouble breathing
A fall, a car accident, or a blow to the belly

These signs matter up to a year after birth as well, not only during pregnancy.

Mental health crisis support

Free, confidential, and open around the clock. Feeling unable to cope is reason enough to call, and so is not being sure whether it counts.

988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

Call or text 988 for thoughts of self-harm, hopelessness, or any emotional crisis. Available 24/7 across the United States.

Call 988

Crisis Text Line

Text HOME to 741741 to reach a trained crisis counselor by message, if talking out loud is not possible right now.

Text 741741

Postpartum Support International

A helpline for pregnancy and postpartum mental health, including anxiety, depression, and intrusive thoughts. Call or text 1-800-944-4773.

Call the helpline

Your OB/GYN or midwife

Most practices have a 24-hour line for urgent questions. Worth saving in your phone now, before you need it at 2 a.m.

What to say when you call

  • How many weeks pregnant you are, or how long since you gave birth.
  • What you are feeling, and when it started.
  • Whether it is getting worse, and anything that changes it.
  • That you want to be evaluated. You do not have to justify the call.
