Healthy Habits

Simple habits for a healthier pregnancy

Explore gentle, practical guidance across six core areas of pregnancy wellness. Pick a topic below to get started.

01

Fill half your plate with produce

Aim for colorful fruits and vegetables at most meals for fiber, vitamins, and steady energy.

02

Pair carbs with protein

Adding protein or healthy fat to carb-heavy snacks helps steady blood sugar and curb energy crashes.

03

Don't skip breakfast

Starting the day with a balanced meal can ease nausea and help prevent energy dips later on.

04

Ask about a prenatal vitamin

A daily prenatal with folic acid supports your baby's early development — ask your provider which one is right for you.

01

Keep water within reach

A bottle on your desk or nightstand makes sipping throughout the day feel effortless.

02

Flavor it naturally

Add citrus, berries, or mint if plain water starts to feel unappealing.

03

Watch for thirst cues

Dizziness, dark urine, or headaches can be early signs your body needs more fluids.

04

Balance your caffeine

Most providers suggest keeping caffeine moderate; pregnancy-safe herbal teas can be a soothing swap.

01

Start with short walks

Even 10–15 minutes a day supports circulation, mood, and energy levels.

02

Try prenatal yoga or stretching

Gentle stretching eases tension in the back, hips, and shoulders as your body changes.

03

Swim if it feels good

Water supports your weight and can be a relaxing, low-impact way to stay active.

04

Always check with your provider

Get the green light for exercise, especially if you have any pregnancy complications.

01

Sleep on your side

Later in pregnancy, side-sleeping — especially the left side — supports healthy blood flow to baby.

02

Use pillows for support

A pillow between your knees or under your belly can ease pressure and help you settle in.

03

Wind down consistently

A calming routine — dim lights, no screens — signals to your body that it's time to rest.

04

Nap without guilt

Short daytime rests can help make up for interrupted nighttime sleep.

01

Name what you're feeling

Journaling or talking things through can make big emotions feel more manageable.

02

Build a support circle

Lean on partners, friends, family, or a prenatal group who can listen without judgment.

03

Practice small moments of calm

A few minutes of deep breathing or quiet can lower stress right in the moment.

04

Reach out if it feels heavy

Persistent sadness or anxiety is common and treatable — your provider can help connect you with support.

01

Keep every appointment

Regular visits let your provider closely track both your health and your baby's development.

02

Bring a running question list

Jot questions down as you think of them so nothing gets forgotten at your next visit.

03

Know your screening timeline

Ask your provider which tests happen in each trimester, and why they matter.

04

Loop in your support person

Having someone join appointments can help you remember details and feel more supported.

A gentle reminder: These tips are general wellness information, not medical advice. Every pregnancy is different — always check with your doctor or midwife before making changes to your diet, exercise, or supplement routine.
