About the project

Pregnancy wellness tracking, scoped on purpose

Happy Moms is a student systems engineering project: a web-based wellness platform for expecting mothers that stays firmly outside clinical care.

Mission

Make the interval between visits legible

Pregnancy creates a nine-month need for longitudinal awareness, but the weeks between appointments are exactly where detail gets lost. By the time someone is in the exam room, the bad sleep stretch three weeks ago and the headaches that came with it are hard to reconstruct.

Happy Moms pairs very short daily check-ins with light engagement, so a record accumulates without feeling like work. It then summarizes that record into notes a mother can choose to share with her provider. The value is in noticing and remembering, not in interpreting.

The problem, in numbers

What the research says

Only about 25% tracked consistently

In a prospective cohort of 585 pregnant women using a digital health platform, roughly a quarter completed the requested health assessments consistently, while 63% missed at least one entirely. Sustained engagement, not data capture, is the hard part.

Brusniak, K., Arndt, H. M., Feisst, M., Haßdenteufel, K., Matthies, L. M., Deutsch, T. M., Hudalla, H., Abele, H., Wallwiener, M., & Wallwiener, S. (2020). Challenges in acceptance and compliance in digital health assessments during pregnancy: Prospective cohort study. JMIR mHealth and uHealth, 8(10), e17377.

72.9% searched online for health information

Among U.S. pregnant women surveyed in 2018, nearly three quarters reported seeking health information online, and the authors specifically flagged concerns about safety, accuracy, and reliability. Add chatbots that hallucinate, and the case for constrained answers gets stronger.

Hsiao, S.-H., Hsu, H.-Y., Erickson, S. R., Lu, T.-S., & Wu, C.-H. (2023). Seeking health information online among U.S. pregnant women: Findings from the 2009–2018 National Health Interview Surveys. Women & Health, 63(1), 59–69.

System boundary

What is inside the system, and what is not

Because pregnancy wellness sits so close to clinical care, defining this boundary was the first job, not an afterthought.

Inside

  • Baseline wellness profile and due date tracking
  • Recurring daily wellness check-ins
  • Engagement activities such as the daily puzzle
  • Wellness information from approved sources
  • The guardrailed wellness assistant
  • Longitudinal trends over the logged history
  • Appointment summary generation, controlled by the mother

Outside

  • Clinical assessment, diagnosis, prescribing, and treatment
  • Emergency and crisis response services, which we only point to
  • Electronic health records and provider clinical systems
  • The underlying AI model, which we constrain but do not build
  • The phone or computer used to reach the app

The mother stays in control. Happy Moms never transmits information to a provider, a family member, or a health record on its own. A summary is produced for her, and she decides whether and how it is shared.

Stakeholders

Who this has to work for

Expecting mothers

The primary users. They need trustworthy answers at any hour, tracking that costs almost no effort, and a clear voice at appointments.

Partners and family

A support network that wants to help without overstepping. Shareable activities give them a way in while the mother keeps control of her information.

OB/GYN providers

Secondary users who read the summary. They need a concise, accurate account of the interval that adds no liability and does not disrupt a short visit.

Prenatal care coordinators

Often the ones who introduce the tool. Onboarding has to fit existing intake and scheduling without new training burden.

Crisis support services

External partners who receive escalations. Referrals have to be appropriate and well timed, neither over- nor under-triggered.

Us, maintaining it

Content changes as guidance changes. Tips, assistant answers, and puzzle words all live in one file so updates stay a small job.

Roadmap

Built incrementally, on purpose

Rather than attempting the full capability at once, the plan starts with the parts that drive engagement and adds the heavier machinery once the habit holds.

Shipped

The tracking core

Baseline profile, daily check-ins, trends and coverage, visit notes, the guardrailed assistant over vetted content, the daily puzzle, 45 tip cards, urgent help routing, dark mode, and full data export and delete.

Next

Accounts and sharing

A partner view with its own permissions, cross-device sync, and reminder notifications. All three need a backend and an authentication story, which this free static deployment intentionally avoids for now.

Later

Assisted summaries and the fourth trimester

A language model rephrasing the same approved sources behind the same screening, richer trend synthesis, and a full postpartum mode. A basic fourth trimester toggle already exists in your profile.

How it is built

Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript

No framework, no build step, no server. The whole app is static files hosted on GitHub Pages, and every entry is stored in your own browser.

Why no backend

Health notes are sensitive. Keeping them on the device means there is no database to breach, no account to manage, and no hosting bill, which suits a course project that still has to be genuinely usable.

What that costs

No sync between devices, no automatic partner access, and no push reminders. Clearing your browser data clears your history, so the export button matters.

Content in one place

Tips, assistant answers, urgent-sign lists, and puzzle words are all data in a single file, so updating guidance does not mean touching the interface.
